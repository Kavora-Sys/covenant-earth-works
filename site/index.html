01  /  The Problem

From the plains to the foothills,
the ground doesn't hold still.

Winter heaves a gravel road six inches and spring takes it back. Clay holds water against a foundation instead of letting it drain away from it. Organic soil worth saving gets buried under spoil nobody tracked, and a year later there is nothing left to build with.

Most of what goes wrong on a site here was decided before the first machine showed up in a soil cut nobody checked, a drainage plan drawn for dry weather, or a schedule that ignored breakup.

So we start underground. Strip topsoil and stockpile it somewhere it can be reused. Bench and cut to drain. Build base in lifts that compact properly. Put culverts where the water goes, not where the drawing said.

And we schedule around the freeze–thaw cycle instead of arguing with it. The work is unglamorous. It is the difference between a road that lasts ten years and one that washes out the first June.

  • 01
    Clay doesn't forgive

    Red Deer-area clays hold water and shrink as they dry. A pad graded dead flat to the design is a pad that ponds. We cut a grade that moves water off the site before it moves anything else.

  • 02
    Topsoil is the asset, not the waste

    Stripped organic soil is worth more than the dirt beneath it. Nobody trucks it off your property or buries it under spoil.

  • 03
    Freeze–thaw sets the schedule

    Roads in this climate fail in spring, not in summer. Base depth, compaction effort and timing decide whether a road survives June. You do not get to choose the weather; you plan around it.

  • 04
    Water goes somewhere

    Every site either drains or ponds. Catch basins, swales and culverts get placed with the grade. Bolting them on at the end leaves nowhere to put them.

07  /  Straight Answers

Questions we get asked
before the first call.

Straight answers. If yours isn't here, ask it directly. You'll get a straight answer, including when the answer is no.

What does an earthworks contractor do?

An earthworks contractor moves, removes and reshapes soil and rock to prepare land for construction. That includes clearing vegetation, cutting and filling to a design grade, building roads and pads, installing drainage, and finishing grade so the site can be built on.

What area does Covenant Earth Works serve?

Covenant Earth Works is based in Calgary, Alberta and serves Calgary and Calgary metro including Okotoks, Airdrie, Cochrane, Chestermere and High River, plus Red Deer, Rocky View County, Red Deer County and central Alberta. Travel beyond those areas is available on request.

Who owns Covenant Earth Works?

Covenant Earth Works is owned and operated by Barry Porter, who has 25 years of experience across the construction and logging industries 20 years in construction and 5 years in logging equipment, demolition, grading and operations.

How much equipment does Covenant Earth Works own?

The company owns an excavator and has access to other construction equipment on demand, including a grader and dump trucks. Equipment is maintained in-house.

When is the best time to grade in central Alberta?

Bulk earthworks and road construction are generally scheduled to finish before sustained freezing, and fine grading is completed outside spring thaw. Freeze–thaw conditions mean roads fail in spring rather than summer, so base course depth, compaction and timing determine whether a road survives June.

Do you install drainage and culverts?

Yes. Drainage goes into the grade as construction proceeds: swales, ditching, catch basins and retention, with culverts sized for actual flow. Catch basin inverts are set from finished grade rather than raw grade so they catch what they're meant to.

Do you work on residential acreage?

Yes. Residential and commercial site work includes site preparation, rough and fine grading, driveways and approaches, culverts, drainage, trenching and pond excavation.

Do you pour concrete or lay asphalt?

No. Covenant Earth Works is an earthworks and site-work contractor. It does not pour concrete, lay asphalt, frame buildings or run utility installation crews, though it works alongside those trades and is usually scheduled earlier than they are.

How does the quote process work?

Send the location, scope and timing through the quote form. Covenant Earth Works calls back, walks the site for anything worth pricing properly, then issues a written quote with scope, price and schedule.

How much does site preparation or road building cost?

It depends on volume, access and what you hit underground, so we don't publish numbers. What we do is walk the site, measure the cut and fill, and put a written price and schedule on it. If a fixed budget matters more than the spec, say so up front. We can often phase the work to fit it.

How soon can you start, and how long will it take?

Both depend on where we are in the season and how big the job is. Winter suits bulk earthworks. Frozen ground is stable and compaction fights you less. Fine grading and drainage want to happen outside the thaw. We give you our real availability up front rather than committing to a date we can't hold.
