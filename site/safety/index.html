The Program

Documented, not
implied.

A written safety program is a legal baseline for every Alberta contractor. What matters is whether it's used on a Tuesday afternoon when the schedule is behind.

01 / Orientation

Site-specific, every site

Nobody works a new site without a site-specific orientation covering ground conditions, access, buried services, overhead power and the hazards specific to that location. Visitors included.

02 / Competency

Trained for the machine

Operators hold the certification for the equipment they run. New hires start supervised, on ground their current ticket covers, with a documented sign-off before working alone.

03 / Verification

Daily checks, documented

Pre-start inspection on every machine, every shift. Anything defective gets tagged and taken out of service rather than "worked around until Friday."

04 / Stop Work

Anyone can stop it

Every person on site has stop-work authority, without needing permission from anyone else. No schedule, no client and no lost day is a reason to override it.

05 / Reporting

Incidents and near misses

Incidents, near misses and hazards are recorded and reviewed rather than quietly ignored. A close call is free information. Capture it before someone gets hurt.

06 / Emergency

A plan before the shift

First aid on site and trained first aiders present. Emergency contacts, muster points and the site-specific response brief before the first machine starts, not after.

Site Hazards

The hazards that
show up here.

Generic safety copy doesn't help anyone make a decision on a Tuesday. These are the risks specific to earthworks across this part of Alberta, and how each one is managed.

  • 01
    Unstable cut faces & benching

    Cuts benched and battered to the angle the material holds, inspected as we go and after rain. Spoil kept back from the crest of the edge by a set distance, never parked where it can load the face.

  • 02
    Buried and overhead services

    Located and marked before any excavation. Exposed services identified, flagged and kept clear. We find out where a line runs before we dig.

  • 03
    Mobile equipment & separation

    Controlled access zones around excavators, dozers and loaders, with pedestrians kept out of swing radius and reversing areas. Spotters used where sightlines are restricted.

  • 04
    Weather and visibility

    We stop when conditions exceed people's limits: whiteout, blowing dust, or cold that affects both the controls and compaction.

  • 05
    Public and residential interface

    When we're working alongside occupied homes or an active road, public protection and clear signage come first. Speed limits enforced on site, access maintained for residents.

  • 06
    Trenches and excavations

    Shoring, sloping or benched as required, with spoil and equipment kept back from the edge by the required distance. Inspections before entry, any time conditions change.

Documentation

02

WCB Alberta

Registered and current with Workers' Compensation Board – Alberta. Clearance provided on request for tender and pre-qualification purposes.

03

ISNetworld

Contractor pre-qualification where required, with our current declarations available to clients who need them for their own compliance.

04

Worker education

First Aid, WHMIS, forklift and equipment-specific certifications current for everyone on crew. An expired ticket means the seat, not the machine.
